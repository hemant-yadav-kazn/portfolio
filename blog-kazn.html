Agency Log: KAZN

Building the
Vibe Code.

"A brand doesn't need a louder voice. It needs a more honest one."

I started KAZN because I kept watching health and wellness brands make the same mistake: shouting louder instead of connecting deeper. Bigger ad spend, more posts, more noise—and somehow, less trust with every push.

Hospitality had already taught me the opposite approach. A great server doesn't talk more; they read the room and say the RIGHT THING at the right moment. I wanted to build an agency that treated digital marketing the same way—less megaphone, more conversation.

The lab where operations meets marketing

What a "Vibe Code" Actually Is

Every brand has an identity on paper—a logo, a color palette, a mission statement. Almost none of them have translated that identity into how it actually FEELS to interact with, whether that's a website, a DM reply, or a checkout flow.

A Vibe Code is that translation layer. It's the set of decisions—tone of voice, pacing, visual rhythm, even response time—that make a digital brand feel like a person you'd trust rather than a company trying to sell you something. Get it right, and a wellness brand's website feels like a knowledgeable friend instead of a sales funnel.

Practically, that means I spend as much time auditing a client's actual customer conversations as I do building their campaigns. AI tools now do the heavy lifting on research and drafting; my job is making sure none of it sounds like a robot wrote it.

AI drafts it. Judgment decides if it ships.

Operations Is the Real Advantage

Most agencies start with creative and bolt on process later, if ever. I built KAZN the other way around, because the same SOPs and workflow discipline that keep a 500-cover dinner service from falling apart are exactly what keep a small agency from missing deadlines and losing client trust.

That's the actual differentiator: I'm not just another marketer with opinions about hooks and headlines. I'm someone who's run high-stakes operations under pressure, and I bring that same rigor to campaign logistics—content calendars that actually hold, feedback loops that close, and a founder who answers emails like a service professional, not a creative who's "in the zone."

KAZN isn't finished. It's still the lab. But the experiments are starting to look like a business.
